Create a publication-quality Nature-style scientific graphical abstract for a
materials-discovery paper. This is a conceptual workflow schematic, not a data
figure.

Canvas and visual hierarchy:
- Wide 16:9 landscape canvas with a clean white background and generous whitespace.
- One continuous left-to-right visual narrative, not a dashboard and not a grid of
  software-style cards.
- The central three-axis semantic-fingerprint region is the visual hero and occupies
  about half of the canvas.
- Flat vector-like scientific illustration with subtle depth only on molecules and
  material interfaces. Thin precise arrows, consistent line weight, refined spacing,
  and a restrained Nature-family palette: charcoal and cool grey scaffolding, muted
  rose for NCM811, teal for lithium metal, muted violet for LHCE solvation, and a
  small amber accent for final aggregation.

Scientific flow:
1. At far left, show a diverse library of small neutral organic molecular structures,
   represented as many different skeletal formulas and molecule glyphs. Label this
   group exactly "800 candidates". Let the molecular library converge through a
   clean screening funnel labelled exactly "Stage 1", ending at a compact group
   labelled exactly "Top 200".
2. Transform the shortlisted molecules into a single elegant structure-explicit
   molecular-card motif containing a skeletal structure, a canonical SMILES line,
   molecular formula, molecular weight, polarity, flexibility and functional-group
   descriptors. Label it exactly "Molecular card". Nearby, show ten small reference
   additive molecules as a balanced anchor constellation labelled exactly
   "10 anchors"; do not make any one anchor visually dominant.
3. In the central hero region, route the molecular card into a refined chemistry
   language-model motif labelled exactly "ChemDFM". Show hidden-state extraction as
   an abstract high-dimensional vector or semantic fingerprint. Make it visually
   explicit that no text is generated by including the short label exactly
   "No generation".
4. From ChemDFM, branch smoothly into three parallel scientific environments:
   - top, muted rose: a layered NCM811 cathode particle and protective interface,
     labelled exactly "NCM811";
   - middle, teal: a lithium-metal surface with uniform ion flux and a thin stable
     interface, labelled exactly "Li metal";
   - bottom, muted violet: a LiFSI/DME/TTE localized high-concentration solvation
     aggregate with coordinated lithium ions, solvent and anions, labelled exactly
     "LHCE".
5. Within each branch, show a faint empty/no-molecule reference signal being
   subtracted from the molecule-containing hidden vector, followed by a clean
   normalization symbol. Represent this visually with paired translucent vectors,
   subtraction, then a unit-length fingerprint. Do not use a long equation.
6. For each branch, compare the centered fingerprint with the same constellation of
   ten anchor fingerprints. Highlight only the two nearest anchor connections and
   merge them into one axis score. Use the short label exactly "Top-2 similarity".
7. At the right, convert the three axis scores into three equal visual contributions
   that converge symmetrically. Label the convergence exactly "Equal weight". The
   combined stream ends in a clean ordered stack of anonymous molecular candidates
   labelled exactly "Final ranking".
8. Add one visually secondary dashed side path containing a small circular molecular
   fingerprint motif labelled exactly "ECFP: ties only". This dashed path may touch
   only the final ordering step and must clearly not enter the three-axis semantic
   score.

Scientific constraints:
- The workflow must stop at deterministic final ranking.
- Do not show a target molecule, a named winning molecule, target registration,
  blind reveal, result unlocking, experimental validation, battery cycling curves,
  quantitative performance values, p-values, spectra, microscopy, logos or journal
  marks.
- Do not imply that Stage 1 scores or ECFP are averaged into the semantic score.
- Do not add mechanisms or experimental claims beyond the described workflow.
- All final candidate identities must remain generic and anonymous.

Typography and finish:
- Use a modern compact sans-serif typeface with strong hierarchy and excellent
  spacing. Render only the ten quoted labels and spell each label exactly as written.
- Avoid paragraphs, legends, decorative frames, oversized icons, glossy 3D rendering,
  gradients, drop shadows, clip-art aesthetics and crowded nested boxes.
- The result should feel like a cohesive high-impact chemistry-paper graphical
  abstract: scientifically precise, elegant, airy and immediately readable.
